Get it on your phone or tablet

Install in four steps

SeeBeeArr for Android is a direct download (an APK file) rather than a Play Store app, so you’ll allow a one-time install from your browser. It takes about a minute.

  1. Download the app

    On your Android phone or tablet, tap Download for Android. You’ll get a file named SeeBeeArr.apk.

    Wi-Fi is a good idea for the download.

  2. Open the file

    Tap it in the download notification, or find it in Files › Downloads and tap it.

  3. Allow the install

    Android asks to allow installs from your browser or Files app: tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, go back, then tap Install.

    If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, tap More details › Install anyway.

  4. Open SeeBeeArr

    Tap Open. Your shelves start with a free sample comic, Little Nemo in Slumberland (1905). Then add your own comics and books, as described below.

    SeeBeeArr won’t ask you for any permissions. It only reads the files and folders you give it.

Inside the app

A bookcase that reads with you

Comics and books

CBZ, CBR (including RAR 5), CB7, CBT and PDF comics, plus EPUB, MOBI, LIT, FB2, text, Markdown, HTML and RTF books.

Real shelves

Covers stand on wooden shelves, and each folder is a stack of books. Touch and hold any cover for its full title.

Sideways spreads

Spreads drawn sideways, and scans that are upside down, are spotted by their lettering and turned for you.

Fit pages to screen

Optionally turn wide spreads on their side so every page fills the screen, with the tap zones turning too.

Read straight through

Finish an issue and the next one in the folder opens. A % or a check on each cover shows how far you’ve read.

Private by design

No account, ads or tracking. Your files stay on your device, and SeeBeeArr reopens the folder you were last in.

Adding comics and books

The quickest way: in Gmail, Messages, Chrome, your Files app or a cloud drive app, open or select a comic or book, tap Share (or Open with) and choose SeeBeeArr. SeeBeeArr adds it to your library and opens it.

To add many files at once, use any of these:

  1. Add Files

    On the Library screen, tap ⋮Add Files…, pick one or more files (touch and hold to select several) and tap Select. They’re copied into SeeBeeArr’s own folder.

  2. Add a folder you already use

    Tap ⋮ › Add Folder…, open a folder holding your comics (on the device, an SD card or a cloud drive), tap Use this folder and then Allow. Folders inside it are included too.

  3. Use the Files app

    SeeBeeArr’s folder appears in your device’s Files app, in the side menu, as SeeBeeArr. Copy or move comics into it and make folders to organize them.

Downloads from Chrome go to your Download folder: tap Open when one finishes and choose SeeBeeArr, or add a folder such as Download › Comics once and save your downloads there. The ? button in the app has a full guide, including copying files from a computer.

Common questions

Android says “Can’t use this folder. To protect your privacy, choose another folder.”

That message comes from Android, not SeeBeeArr. Since Android 11, no app may be given the top level of your storage: the root of an SD card, the root of the device’s storage, or the Download folder itself.

Choose a folder inside instead. For example, make a folder named Comics on your SD card (or inside Download) in the Files app, move your comics into it, then add that folder with ⋮ › Add Folder…. You can also add several folders one at a time; each becomes its own stack on the shelves.

A file won’t open. What can I do?

SeeBeeArr shows a message explaining why. The usual causes:

  • Copy protection (DRM). Most books bought from Google Play Books, Kindle and other stores are protected and can’t be opened by other apps.
  • An incomplete download. If a site needed you to sign in first, the “comic” may really be a saved web page. Download it again.
  • A file in a cloud drive. It may need to download the first time you open it, which needs an internet connection.

If none of these fit, email me the message you see.

Why did a page turn sideways or upside down?

Some comics have two-page spreads drawn sideways, and some scans are upside down. SeeBeeArr reads the lettering to spot these and turns the page so it reads correctly. If it gets one wrong, tap the middle of the page, then the rotate button at the top, and choose Rotate Right or Rotate Left; SeeBeeArr remembers your choice. You can switch automatic turning off in the same menu.

Can pages turn to fit the screen instead of the screen turning?

Yes. In the rotate menu, switch on Fit Pages to Screen. Any page that doesn’t match the shape of your screen is turned so it fills it: hold your device upright, and when a wide spread comes up, turn the device to the left to read it. Tapping the page’s own left or right edge still turns pages: the tap zones turn with the page.

While it’s on, SeeBeeArr keeps the screen from turning. Some large tablets don’t allow apps to do that; on those, also turn off Auto-rotate in the quick settings (swipe down from the top of the screen).

How do I turn pages and zoom?

Tap the right edge of the screen for the next page and the left edge for the previous one, or swipe. Tap the middle to show or hide the controls. In comics, spread two fingers apart to zoom in on a panel.

In a folder of comics or books, turning past the last page opens the next one in the folder, so you can read a whole series straight through. A book you’ve started shows how far you’ve read on its cover, such as 42%, and a green check once you’ve finished it. Touch and hold a cover and choose Mark as Read or Mark as Unread to change it.

How do I delete a comic or book?

Touch and hold the cover, choose Delete, then confirm. This works for files in SeeBeeArr’s own folder and in folders you added. For an added folder, the file is deleted from that folder (for example, from your SD card), not just hidden, and the confirmation says so.

If Delete doesn’t appear for a book in an added folder, open ⋮ › Manage Folders, remove that folder and add it again, and allow access when Android asks. Removing a folder from SeeBeeArr never deletes any files.

Can SeeBeeArr open Kindle books?

It opens older Kindle-format files without copy protection (MOBI, PRC, AZW). Newer Kindle-only formats (AZW3, KFX) and protected Kindle purchases can’t be opened.

Where can I find free comics?

The Digital Comic Museum has thousands of public-domain Golden Age comics, free and legal to download (a free account is needed). SeeBeeArr also comes with the first pages of Winsor McCay’s Little Nemo in Slumberland (1905).

Will I lose my place if I uninstall the app?

Reading positions are stored only on your device, so uninstalling SeeBeeArr removes them, along with any files in SeeBeeArr’s own folder. Copy files you want to keep somewhere else with the Files app first. Files in folders you added stay where they are. Updating SeeBeeArr keeps everything.

Privacy Policy

SeeBeeArr collects nothing

Effective November 4, 2026

What we collect

Nothing. SeeBeeArr does not collect, store, sell or share any personal information. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking, and it does not send your data to the developer or to anyone else.

Your files

Your comics and books stay on your device, or in the folders you choose to add, such as a folder on an SD card or in a cloud drive. SeeBeeArr only opens the files and folders you give it, and only to display them, or to delete one when you ask it to. Covers, reading positions and page rotations are saved on your device so the app can show your library and remember your place. They are removed if you uninstall the app.

Text recognition

To spot sideways and upside-down pages, SeeBeeArr uses Google’s ML Kit text recognition, which runs entirely on your device: page images are never sent anywhere. As part of Google’s ML Kit, this component may send Google basic diagnostic and usage information about how it performs (for example, performance and error data), as described in the ML Kit terms. It contains nothing from your files.

Cloud drives and other apps

If you keep files in a cloud drive or open them from another app, your device gets them from that service or app under your own account. SeeBeeArr has no access to your account and receives nothing from it.

Links to other websites

The app’s Help screen links to other websites, such as the Digital Comic Museum. Those sites open in your web browser and have their own privacy policies.

Children

SeeBeeArr collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes
